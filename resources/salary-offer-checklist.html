Understand your salary and job offer

Compare guaranteed pay, conditions and the cost of doing the job before accepting. This worksheet helps you ask clear questions about an offer without relying on a headline salary.

By Plan B Careers. Published 8 November 2026.

What is the difference between CTC, gross and take-home pay?

CTC describes the employer’s stated total annual cost of your compensation. Gross pay is your pay before employee deductions. Take-home pay is the amount paid after deductions. Ask for the actual breakdown: a salary headline alone does not tell you your guaranteed monthly bank credit.

Terms to clarify in the written offer
TermWhat to check
Annual CTCWhich components are cash salary, employer contributions, insurance, benefits or variable amounts?
Fixed payWhich components are guaranteed under the offer, and are the figures monthly or annual?
Variable pay or incentivesWhat targets, eligibility rules, payment dates and clawback conditions apply? Is any amount guaranteed?
Gross monthly payWhat is paid before employee deductions in a regular month?
Estimated take-home payWhat deductions will apply to your circumstances? Ask payroll for an explanation.
ReimbursementsAre these repayments of approved expenses, and what evidence must you submit?

Why dividing CTC by 12 can be misleading

An annual CTC of ₹2,40,000 divided by 12 is ₹20,000. That calculation is a monthly equivalent of the stated cost, not a promise of ₹20,000 credited to your account. The offer may include non-cash benefits, employer contributions or performance-linked amounts.

Illustrative arithmetic, not a salary benchmark: If an offer states ₹18,000 monthly gross and the itemised employee deductions total ₹1,500, the resulting take-home would be ₹16,500. Actual components and deductions depend on the offer and your circumstances. Ask the employer to confirm them.

For an Akola office role, estimate the actual commute from your home and whether transport is available at the shift’s end. For relocation to Nagpur or Amravati, use current accommodation and travel quotes. For remote work, confirm who provides the laptop, connectivity and any backup requirements.

Work out what remains after job-related costs

Use this comparison formula: estimated take-home minus your additional work-related costs. Keep one-time joining or moving costs separate from monthly costs. The result helps compare affordability; it does not include every household expense.

Fictional comparison: A role with ₹18,000 estimated take-home and ₹2,000 in additional monthly travel costs leaves ₹16,000 before other expenses. A different role with ₹20,000 take-home and ₹6,000 in additional rent and travel costs leaves ₹14,000. These figures demonstrate the method and are not local salary or living-cost estimates.

Questions to ask before accepting

  • Who is the legal employer, and whose payroll will I join?
  • Is the advertised amount CTC, gross pay, take-home or a maximum that includes incentives?
  • What amount is fixed, and what targets apply to the variable part?
  • Are training and probation paid? Does the salary change after either period?
  • What are the work location, hours, weekly days off and joining date?
  • What deductions, deposits, service charges or equipment expenses are being requested, by whom and for what purpose?
  • Will all agreed terms appear in the written offer, with the employer’s contact details?

Ask for a written explanation of any service fee separately from the employer’s salary offer. Check the scope, receipt and refund terms before paying. A payment or registration cannot guarantee that an employer will select you.

How do I discuss salary politely?

Explain your relevant experience and ask about the approved range for the role. A useful wording is: “Thank you for the offer. Could you share the fixed monthly gross, the expected deductions and the incentive conditions? Based on the responsibilities and my experience, is there room to discuss the fixed component?”

Keep the discussion specific. Avoid inventing another offer or overstating your previous pay. If the fixed amount cannot change, clarify training, schedule, location or equipment arrangements that affect your decision.
