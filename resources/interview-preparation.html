Interview preparation for freshers

Prepare for the work in the vacancy, practise honest examples and leave the interview with clear next steps. Use this guide for a first job, a career restart or a move into a new role.

By Plan B Careers. Published 8 November 2026.

How should a fresher prepare for an interview?

Start with the job description. Identify the work you would do, choose two real examples that show relevant skills, and practise a short introduction. Confirm the interview location or meeting link, the required documents and the person you will meet.

  1. Match the role. For customer support, practise listening and explaining a solution. For sales, practise asking questions and explaining a product. For data entry, show accuracy using a task you have actually completed.
  2. Research the employer. Check the company website and independently confirm the recruiter’s contact. Read the role’s location, shift and pay conditions. Ask about any missing details before travelling.
  3. Choose evidence. A college project, family-business task, volunteering assignment or previous job can show responsibility. Describe your own contribution accurately.
  4. Practise aloud. Record a short answer, listen once and remove repeated phrases. Use clear language you can sustain in a conversation.
  5. Prepare the basics. Keep your resume, a notebook and the requested documents ready. For a video interview, test audio, the meeting app, charging and your internet connection.

How do I answer “Tell me about yourself”?

Use a simple sequence: your current situation, one relevant strength supported by an example, and why this role fits your next step. Aim for a focused introduction rather than reciting every line of your resume.

Illustrative structure, not a script to copy: “I recently completed my degree in Akola. During a college event, I maintained the registration sheet and helped visitors with their questions. I am interested in this customer-support role because it involves careful record keeping and clear communication.”

Replace every detail with your own experience. If you have not done the activity, choose another example. You can practise in the language you are most comfortable with, then confirm the employer’s language requirements.

Use a clear example when you have no work experience

The STAR structure helps explain an example: Situation, Task, Action and Result. Spend most of your answer on what you did. Results can be a completed task or a lesson; you do not need to invent a number.

SituationWhere did the task happen, and what problem needed attention?

Questions worth practising

  • Why do you want this role? Connect one responsibility in the vacancy to an interest or skill you can demonstrate.
  • How would you handle an upset customer? Explain how you would listen, clarify the issue, follow the company’s process and ask for help when needed.
  • What do you do when you make a mistake? Give a real example of identifying it, correcting it and reducing the chance of repeating it.
  • Are you comfortable with the shift or location? State your actual availability. Ask for the schedule and reporting location before agreeing.
  • What salary do you expect? Ask what fixed pay, variable pay and deductions the role includes. Use the salary and offer checklist before deciding.
  • Why is there a gap in your resume? Give a brief factual explanation and describe your current readiness. Keep private details private.

What should I ask the interviewer?

  • What would I be expected to do in my first month?
  • Who would I report to, and how is performance assessed?
  • What are the exact work location, shifts, weekly days off and training arrangements?
  • What is the fixed monthly pay, what depends on targets, and when will I receive a written offer?
  • What is the next step, and when should I follow up?

After the interview, note the recruiter’s name, the date and the agreed next step. A short follow-up can say: “Thank you for discussing the role with me. I remain interested. Please let me know if you need any further information.”
